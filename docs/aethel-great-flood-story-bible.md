# AETHEL: WHEN THE HORIZON ROSE
## Ensemble story bible for a world approaching the Great Flood

> **Form:** Four-volume epic or four-season prestige drama  
> **Span:** The final 420 days of the old world  
> **Cast:** 61 named human characters; every character changes the outcome  
> **Genre:** Deep-time climate epic, political drama, family saga and survival story  
> **Promise:** The audience knows the flood is coming. Most characters do not. The suspense lies in what they will save, whom they will believe, and what kind of people they become when survival cannot be equal.

---

# 1. The central idea

Twenty thousand years ago, four civilizations share a fragile network of trade and knowledge:

- **Orun**, a northern basalt city beside the continental ice.
- **Tavra**, a populous volcanic garden culture on the western coast.
- **Suun**, a floating commonwealth on the immense freshwater Lake Aruun.
- **Kheled**, a southern plateau society of astronomers, mediators and cistern-builders.

For generations the northern ice has held an inland ocean behind it. The Oruni hear new fractures beneath the glacier. Suun sailors report that Lake Aruun is rising although its southern rivers are dry. Kheledi astronomers see that the moon is not responsible, contradicting frightened priests. Tavran wells taste of salt.

The catastrophe is not one impossible wall of water covering the planet. It is a chain:

1. Warm ocean currents undermine the western ice shelf.
2. A glacial lake bursts beneath Orun.
3. The pressure shift triggers earthquakes along Tavra’s volcanic coast.
4. Tsunamis cross the western basin.
5. Lake Aruun breaches its southern rim.
6. Meltwater, storm surge and displaced rivers drown the connected lowlands.
7. The old coast disappears permanently as the climate continues warming.

To those living through it, the separate disasters become **one Flood**.

The old world does not fall because its people are primitive, foolish or sinful. It falls because the scale of change exceeds every institution they possess—and because power decides whose warnings are heard.

## Dramatic question

**When no one can save the whole world, who gets to decide what part of it deserves to continue?**

## Thematic oppositions

- Memory versus truth
- Survival versus justice
- Local loyalty versus human solidarity
- Written record versus embodied knowledge
- Prediction versus persuasion
- A perfect archive versus living, imperfect witnesses
- The desire to be remembered versus the courage to let others inherit the future

# 2. Narrative architecture

The cast is large, but the audience enters through **twelve principal viewpoints**. Sixteen major supporting characters carry complete subplots, while thirty-two constellation characters make the world feel inhabited and alter pivotal events.

## The twelve principal viewpoints

1. **Eira of Orun** — an ice-listener whose correct warning destroys her reputation.
2. **Tovan of Orun** — a disgraced rescue captain who believes courage can repair every mistake.
3. **Nara of Tavra** — a gardener who discovers salt entering the city’s roots.
4. **Ilyon of Tavra** — an adored politician who understands the danger but fears mass panic.
5. **Seli of Suun** — a young navigator able to read a changing lake better than any elder.
6. **Maru of Suun** — a harbour-builder who turns refugee boats away before becoming one.
7. **Aven of Kheled** — a mediator sent to determine whether Orun’s warning is credible.
8. **Tarek of Kheled** — a cistern engineer asked to convert sacred courts into reservoirs.
9. **Oru the Roadless** — a steppe herder whose mammoths begin migrating in the wrong season.
10. **Mara-of-No-Hearth** — an itinerant singer carrying mutually contradictory histories.
11. **Kes** — a child from a drowned, politically invisible marsh community.
12. **Sava** — the archivist ordered to choose one thousand objects from an entire civilization.

## Four-volume progression

### Volume I — *The Sound Beneath the Ice* (Days 420–260)
Warnings appear separately and are explained away separately. The First Accord assembly gathers in Tavra. Eira’s evidence is ridiculed after a manipulated demonstration. Kes’s marsh village is quietly lost in a “minor” surge, producing the first refugees. The volume ends when an entire Oruni listening station vanishes into a new blue crevasse.

### Volume II — *The Argument of Cities* (Days 259–100)
The four cultures debate evacuation, trade restrictions and ownership of high ground. Political factions weaponize uncertainty. Characters cross regions, fall in love, betray mentors and encounter ways of life they had dismissed. A failed attempt to drain the northern lake accelerates the crisis. The volume ends with the moon court voting against compulsory evacuation by one voice.

### Volume III — *Forty Days of Dust* (Days 99–1)
Earthquake swarms close roads. Tavra’s mountain exhales ash, producing darkness rather than rain. Animals migrate. Food prices fracture the Accord. Each settlement builds a different salvation project: Orun’s ice road, Tavra’s cavern fleet, Suun’s reed armada and Kheled’s high refuge. The last day ends in unnatural silence as Lake Aruun drops three arm-lengths in an hour.

### Volume IV — *When the Horizon Rose* (Days 0–20, then Year 1)
The glacial dam fails. The flood arrives in different forms over several days. Plans work imperfectly; supposedly foolish local knowledge saves thousands. Several central characters die, but no death exists merely for spectacle. In the aftermath, survivors discover that the coast will not return. The final struggle concerns who controls the story of what happened. The ending follows multiple survivor groups walking away with conflicting versions—the beginning of the world’s flood myths.

# 3. The sixty-one lives

Each character has four anchors:

- **Surface:** how the world initially sees them.
- **Private story:** the life beneath that impression.
- **Flood choice:** the decision only this catastrophe could force.
- **Arc:** who they become, or fail to become.

# ORUN — THE NORTHERN REACH

## 1. Eira Sen — Listener of the Third Vault · Principal POV
**Personality:** Exacting, dryly funny, physically timid, morally stubborn. She apologizes to furniture after bumping it but will contradict an entire council without blinking.

- **Surface:** A gifted young acoustician who interprets vibrations transmitted through basalt pillars sunk into the glacier.
- **Private story:** Eira’s mother died in a minor ice collapse after trusting an instrument Eira had calibrated. Although no one blamed her, she has spent eleven years treating uncertainty as a personal failure.
- **Flood choice:** When the council demands certainty, she must decide whether to issue a warning based on incomplete evidence and accept responsibility for the chaos it causes.
- **Arc:** She begins believing truth is a measurement untouched by personality. She ends understanding that truth must be carried by trust, story and courage. She survives but loses her hearing during the final rupture, becoming the flood’s great visual chronicler.

## 2. Tovan Ash-Hand — Captain of the Ice Road · Principal POV
**Personality:** Charismatic, impulsive, generous and addicted to being needed. He jokes at funerals because silence frightens him.

- **Surface:** Orun’s most famous rescuer, demoted after leading an unauthorized expedition.
- **Private story:** The expedition saved twelve children but cost the life of his husband, Peleth. Praise feels like theft, and Tovan keeps accepting impossible missions in the hope that one will finally balance the debt.
- **Flood choice:** He can lead a celebrated rescue of trapped nobles or quietly keep the evacuation road open for thousands of strangers.
- **Arc:** He relinquishes heroism as performance. He dies anonymously maintaining a rope crossing, known only to the last refugee whose mitten he fastens.

## 3. Ama Kel — Keeper of Names
**Personality:** Severe, tender, tradition-bound and unexpectedly mischievous with children.

- **Surface:** The elderly authority who decides when names of the dead may be sung.
- **Private story:** Ama’s daughter left Orun for Tavra and has not spoken to her in eighteen years. Ama secretly listens to Tavran trade reports just to hear rumours of her.
- **Flood choice:** Sacred law says the glacial name-caves must never be opened outside Long Night. Opening them could provide a safe tunnel through the ridge.
- **Arc:** She breaks the law she spent her life protecting and discovers that tradition’s purpose is shelter, not obedience. She reunites with her daughter only after both have lost their homes.

## 4. Rusk Vey — First Speaker of Orun
**Personality:** Patient, politically brilliant, conflict-averse and vain about his reputation for calm.

- **Surface:** A consensus leader who has prevented war for twenty-three years.
- **Private story:** Rusk grew up during a false evacuation that killed more people through exposure than the predicted icefall did. He sees panic as the greatest natural disaster.
- **Flood choice:** He suppresses Eira’s first report, then must confess publicly before anyone will trust the second.
- **Arc:** His instinct to prevent fear becomes cowardice. He eventually sacrifices his office and good name, living to become a useful but disliked quartermaster among refugees.

## 5. Pell Orro — Stone Singer
**Personality:** Flirtatious, irreverent, sensory and incapable of discussing grief directly.

- **Surface:** A sculptor who tunes basalt chambers for ceremony.
- **Private story:** Pell is Eira’s estranged father. He abandoned technical work after her mother’s death and hides in beauty because facts remind him of guilt.
- **Flood choice:** He can finish a memorial commissioned by the council or retune the city’s ceremonial hall into a gigantic warning resonator.
- **Arc:** He converts the entire city into an alarm, reconciling art with usefulness. He and Eira forgive each other without agreeing about the past.

## 6. Nemi Blue-Bead — Apprentice Sledwright
**Personality:** Competitive, inventive, insecure and hilariously bad at keeping secrets.

- **Surface:** A seventeen-year-old desperate to become Orun’s youngest master craftsperson.
- **Private story:** Nemi was adopted from a steppe caravan and fears every failure will prove she is a guest rather than Oruni.
- **Flood choice:** She exposes that her master has rejected a safer sled design to protect his prestige.
- **Arc:** She loses the competition but builds low, broad flood sleds that become the evacuation’s essential craft. Her design is later remembered as a “boat from the ice.”

## 7. Hadran Vo — Master Sledwright
**Personality:** Proud, disciplined, paternal and terrified of obsolescence.

- **Surface:** Nemi’s respected teacher and the maker of the First Speaker’s sled.
- **Private story:** Arthritis has begun to ruin his hands. He hides mistakes because his identity and household depend upon being useful.
- **Flood choice:** He must endorse Nemi’s superior design and reveal his decline.
- **Arc:** His pride delays production, costing lives. He does confess, then spends his final days teaching children every technique he knows. He survives as a teacher rather than a master.

## 8. Iso Wren — Keeper of Lamps
**Personality:** Gentle, methodical, superstitious and quietly rebellious.

- **Surface:** A temple attendant responsible for maintaining ancestral lamps.
- **Private story:** Iso does not believe ancestors inhabit flames, but the daily tending gave structure after his infant son died.
- **Flood choice:** Lamp oil could waterproof evacuation hides, but using it extinguishes centuries-old sacred fires.
- **Arc:** He pours out the oil himself, discovering that the ritual mattered because people performed it together. He carries one unlit wick south and begins a new ceremony around remembered light.

## 9. Var Sen — Eira’s Younger Brother
**Personality:** Sociable, opportunistic, affectionate and resentful of living beside genius.

- **Surface:** A charming trader who can acquire anything.
- **Private story:** Var forged Eira’s authorization years earlier to finance the expedition that killed their mother. He has let Eira carry the guilt.
- **Flood choice:** Smugglers offer his family passage in exchange for stolen evacuation maps.
- **Arc:** He initially agrees, then confesses and uses the smuggling network to guide refugees. Eira cannot forgive him immediately. He survives with the painful gift of time to earn it.

## 10. Deya Flint — Cave Physician
**Personality:** Blunt, compassionate, impatient with hierarchy and privately afraid of blood.

- **Surface:** Orun’s finest trauma healer.
- **Private story:** Deya faints at the sight of fresh blood and hides it through elaborate routines. She excels because she understands panic intimately.
- **Flood choice:** During the first collapse, her assistants are gone and no routine remains.
- **Arc:** She openly tells injured people about her fear, asking them to keep her conscious by speaking. Vulnerability becomes a medical technique. She later organizes mobile clinics across cultures.

## 11. Korun Bale — Hunter of the White Plain
**Personality:** Laconic, observant, suspicious and tender toward animals.

- **Surface:** An old hunter who claims cave lions speak through tracks.
- **Private story:** Korun has deliberately missed every lion he was contracted to kill after one led him to shelter during a blizzard.
- **Flood choice:** He follows migrating lions away from the official route and must persuade others to trust animal knowledge over maps.
- **Arc:** His “superstition” discovers the only stable pass. He refuses the title of prophet, insisting the lions saved themselves and humans happened to notice.

## 12. Tiri Two-Coats — Refugee Child
**Personality:** Watchful, literal, acquisitive and capable of startling kindness.

- **Surface:** A ten-year-old notorious for stealing gloves and wearing two coats.
- **Private story:** Tiri survived abandonment once and keeps duplicate possessions because nothing singular feels safe.
- **Flood choice:** She must give her second coat to a hypothermic infant.
- **Arc:** After resisting, she gives not only the coat but the hidden map sewn inside it. She becomes Kes’s closest friend; together they create a child’s geography of lost places.

## 13. Varo Ice-Blessed — Popular Seer
**Personality:** Mesmerizing, perceptive, narcissistic and sincerely spiritual.

- **Surface:** He interprets auroras and assures Orun that the ancestors will protect the faithful.
- **Private story:** Varo began as a fraud but came to believe the comfort he creates is itself divine.
- **Flood choice:** Admitting Eira is right would save followers but destroy the identity that gives their lives meaning.
- **Arc:** He delays, then leads a pilgrimage into danger. At the last moment he abandons prophecy, uses his authority to reverse the crowd and dies helping them cross. History remembers either saint or villain depending on the teller.

## 14. Olan Marr — Gate Recorder
**Personality:** Pedantic, loyal, passive-aggressive and hungry to matter.

- **Surface:** A minor clerk recording every person and load entering Orun.
- **Private story:** He writes tiny fictional adventures in ledger margins in which clerks save kingdoms.
- **Flood choice:** The evacuation order excludes undocumented steppe migrants. Olan can alter the official count.
- **Arc:** He invents an entire “Fifth Hearth” and legally admits four hundred outsiders. His forged ledger becomes the most trusted survivor list, turning fiction into shelter.

## 15. Sura Neth — Mother of the Listening House
**Personality:** Warm, controlling, pragmatic and merciless toward self-pity.

- **Surface:** Director of the communal household where Eira and other listeners were trained.
- **Private story:** She once reported her own sister for falsifying data; the sister was exiled and died. Sura believes love means enforcing standards.
- **Flood choice:** She discovers one of Eira’s alarming readings resulted from a damaged instrument, though the overall warning remains valid.
- **Arc:** Instead of concealing the error, she reveals it and publicly defends the larger conclusion. She learns that credibility comes from exposed imperfection, not spotless authority.

# TAVRA — THE EMBER COAST

## 16. Nara Ved — Keeper of the Bitter Garden · Principal POV
**Personality:** Patient, skeptical, sensuous, introverted and ferociously protective of growing things.

- **Surface:** A terrace gardener more comfortable grafting branches than addressing people.
- **Private story:** Nara cannot have children and resents a culture that praises her as “mother of gardens” to avoid acknowledging her grief.
- **Flood choice:** She has space to preserve either rare ceremonial plants or common food cuttings that poor districts need.
- **Arc:** She chooses food and redesigns seed preservation around living, shared gardens rather than elite vaults. Her legacy is not blood or monuments but orchards planted along the refugee road.

## 17. Ilyon Sarr — Voice of the Copper Table · Principal POV
**Personality:** Charming, strategic, empathetic, indecisive and addicted to approval.

- **Surface:** Tavra’s most beloved civic speaker and architect of the First Accord.
- **Private story:** As a child he survived a crowd crush caused by a false eruption warning. Public calm is both policy and trauma response.
- **Flood choice:** He can order early evacuation and risk thousands dying on ash-choked roads, or wait for certainty.
- **Arc:** He waits too long, then lies that evacuation was always planned. Ultimately he broadcasts his own failures through the mirror network, restoring enough trust for people to move. He survives but is never again allowed office.

## 18. Letha Kel — Copper-Mirror Engineer
**Personality:** Brilliant, impatient, sardonic and emotionally extravagant.

- **Surface:** Designer of the mirror shafts that bring sunlight underground.
- **Private story:** Letha is Ama Kel’s estranged daughter. She left Orun after Ama refused to sing Letha’s wife’s name outside the proper season.
- **Flood choice:** Mirror copper can become signal panels or water tanks, not both.
- **Arc:** She builds a daylight code visible across the coast, then melts her most famous mirror into cistern lining. Reunion with Ama forces both women to confront how grief hardened into principle.

## 19. Beren Sarr — Ilyon’s Husband and Communal Baker
**Personality:** Patient, witty, socially fearless and quietly exhausted.

- **Surface:** The grounded spouse who feeds anyone and punctures Ilyon’s grandeur.
- **Private story:** Beren has managed Ilyon’s crises for years and no longer knows whether care has become erasure of himself.
- **Flood choice:** He can accompany Ilyon to safety or keep the district ovens operating during evacuation.
- **Arc:** He chooses the ovens and refuses to make the choice romantic. He survives separately. Their marriage ends tenderly after the flood because love is not always sufficient repair.

## 20. Chief Gardener Ossa
**Personality:** Commanding, traditional, generous in public and territorial in private.

- **Surface:** Overseer of Tavra’s food terraces.
- **Private story:** Ossa has secretly diverted spring water to hide declining soil fertility and preserve her reputation.
- **Flood choice:** Revealing the failing terraces would support evacuation but prove years of deception.
- **Arc:** She doubles down, causing a terrace collapse. She spends her remaining days digging trapped workers out and gives Nara the full water maps before dying from ash-lung months later.

## 21. Jori Flint-Eye — Obsidian Musician
**Personality:** Theatrical, vain, brave in crowds and frightened when alone.

- **Surface:** A percussionist whose performances can synchronize thousands of workers.
- **Private story:** Jori has progressive hearing loss and strikes harder to feel sound through stone.
- **Flood choice:** The evacuation needs drummers stationed along dangerous tunnels to maintain walking rhythm.
- **Arc:** Jori conducts the exodus by vibration, turning disability into expertise. He lives and later composes music that can be performed without hearing.

## 22. Pava En — Salt Merchant
**Personality:** Shrewd, maternal, profane and deeply class-conscious.

- **Surface:** A wealthy trader accused of profiting from shortages.
- **Private story:** Pava was born in the marshes and erased her accent and family ties to gain Tavran citizenship.
- **Flood choice:** Kes identifies her as an aunt. Acknowledging him would expose that she has denied refugee relatives entry.
- **Arc:** She first rejects him, then opens her warehouses and trade records, revealing stored food. She cannot undo her denial, but spends the flood using every corrupt connection to move marsh families uphill.

## 23. Enna Reed — Midwife of the Lower Terraces
**Personality:** Talkative, reassuring, nosy and unshockable.

- **Surface:** A beloved midwife who knows every household secret.
- **Private story:** Enna records births with knotted cords because lower-terrace children are often omitted from civic rolls.
- **Flood choice:** Evacuation priority depends on official population records. Handing over her cords could save families but expose parents hiding from debt or law.
- **Arc:** She negotiates blanket amnesty in exchange for the true count. During the flood she delivers three children in a mirror chamber and names none of them “After.”

## 24. Cala and the Ash Fox — Animal Keeper
**Personality:** Earnest, literal-minded, easily overwhelmed and intensely loyal.

- **Surface:** A zoo attendant mocked for claiming an elderly fox predicts tremors.
- **Private story:** Cala struggles to interpret human expression but understands minute changes in animal posture and scent.
- **Flood choice:** She releases captive animals against orders, losing the only work where she feels competent.
- **Arc:** Their movement reveals a safe ridge. Cala becomes Korun’s field partner, and their quiet friendship establishes the first cross-cultural animal observatory.

## 25. Dar Olt — Commander of the Sea Wall
**Personality:** Disciplined, honorable, unimaginative and secretly romantic.

- **Surface:** The officer certain Tavra’s ancient wall can withstand any wave.
- **Private story:** Dar writes anonymous love poems about the sea while building structures meant to resist it.
- **Flood choice:** Engineers tell him opening the wall’s gates will flood the harbour but reduce pressure on residential terraces.
- **Arc:** He refuses until he sees the first wall buckle, then opens every gate manually. He dies in the harbour; his water-damaged poems cause later generations to mistake him for a sea priest.

## 26. Simo Varr — Apprentice Politician
**Personality:** Idealistic, ambitious, eloquent and cruel when embarrassed.

- **Surface:** Ilyon’s protégé and public advocate for equal evacuation.
- **Private story:** Simo leaks private conversations to make his speeches seem prophetic.
- **Flood choice:** He obtains the secret list reserving cavern space for civic families and can expose it before a compromise is ready.
- **Arc:** He publishes for glory, triggering a riot that destroys supplies. Humbled, he later becomes a door-counter who personally refuses privileged entry. He survives without regaining admiration.

## 27. Vessa Black-Barley — Fermenter
**Personality:** Boisterous, practical, flirtatious and fatalistic.

- **Surface:** Maker of Tavra’s strongest beer and loudest feast host.
- **Private story:** Vessa has stored grain in forbidden burial caverns for years, technically desecrating ancestors but preventing famine.
- **Flood choice:** She can reveal the stores and face execution or let people starve.
- **Arc:** Her crime saves the city during the ash weeks. She is sentenced anyway, then freed when the court itself floods. She dislikes becoming a symbol and opens a refugee tavern where payment is a story.

## 28. Tal Marr — Child Mirror-Runner
**Personality:** Restless, boastful, imaginative and terrified of deep water.

- **Surface:** A twelve-year-old courier racing messages through light shafts.
- **Private story:** Tal witnessed a friend drown and now lies that he can swim.
- **Flood choice:** The last mirror signal requires crossing a flooded chamber.
- **Arc:** He admits he cannot swim. Another child teaches him with a storage jar as float; they send the signal together. His bravery is asking for help rather than conquering fear alone.

## 29. Rovan of the Red Hands — Potter
**Personality:** Quiet, perfectionist, resentful and profoundly observant.

- **Surface:** Maker of ordinary ember vessels, overshadowed by artist siblings.
- **Private story:** Rovan marks each pot with tiny maps of places where its clay originated. Without telling anyone, he has produced the coast’s finest geographic archive.
- **Flood choice:** He cannot carry thousands of vessels uphill.
- **Arc:** He deliberately breaks them and distributes mapped fragments among refugee groups. Archaeologists of the future will mistake them for a unified sacred script.

## 30. Mele Ossa — Terrace Thief
**Personality:** Defiant, funny, suspicious of kindness and morally precise.

- **Surface:** A fifteen-year-old caught stealing medicinal moss.
- **Private story:** Mele supports two younger siblings while their mother works off water debt. She steals only from terraces that falsify yield reports.
- **Flood choice:** Offered amnesty to guide officials through poor districts, she must decide whether collaboration is betrayal.
- **Arc:** She accepts but demands families follow before officials. Mele later becomes the youngest member of the refugee ration council and never stops stealing from unfair piles.

# SUUN — THE INLAND SEA

## 31. Seli Nav — Navigator of the Small Wake · Principal POV
**Personality:** Curious, precise, playful, stubborn and socially impatient.

- **Surface:** A nineteen-year-old navigator whose unconventional route readings embarrass senior pilots.
- **Private story:** Seli experiences numbers as shapes and currents as moving colour. Elders praise the results while calling her explanations childish.
- **Flood choice:** She discovers all official charts assume a stable shoreline and must abandon inherited routes during the largest migration in Suun history.
- **Arc:** She stops translating her mind into forms others respect and teaches crews to see through movement, sound and timing. She guides the armada but cannot save her own father’s boat.

## 32. Maru Drenn — Master of the Eastern Harbour · Principal POV
**Personality:** Efficient, stern, protective and capable of rationalizing anything as necessity.

- **Surface:** The administrator who keeps Suun’s chaotic flotillas orderly.
- **Private story:** Maru survived a childhood famine caused when refugees overwhelmed his village. He equates closed gates with responsible care.
- **Flood choice:** He turns Kes’s marsh boats away, then days later needs Tavra to admit his own people.
- **Arc:** Experiencing rejection breaks his certainty. He returns during the flood to dismantle his harbour and uses its timber to rescue the very marsh community he excluded. Kes never absolves him, and Maru learns repair does not purchase forgiveness.

## 33. Tem Nav — Seli’s Father
**Personality:** Warm, cautious, traditional and passive beneath apparent gentleness.

- **Surface:** A respected navigator proud of his gifted daughter.
- **Private story:** Tem repeatedly presents Seli’s discoveries as his own so councils will accept them, telling himself he is protecting her.
- **Flood choice:** He must publicly credit her and surrender command.
- **Arc:** He delays until Seli challenges him. Later, disobeying her route causes his boat’s loss. He saves passengers but drowns, leaving Seli with anger that cannot be resolved by a noble final act.

## 34. Iri-of-Seven-Boats — Flotilla Mother
**Personality:** Hospitable, manipulative, affectionate and politically relentless.

- **Surface:** Elder of a huge raft-household and broker of marriages.
- **Private story:** Iri’s flotilla is nearly bankrupt. She arranges relationships partly to gain access to mooring rights.
- **Flood choice:** She can preserve her seven linked boats or cut them apart so each can carry refugees through narrow channels.
- **Arc:** She severs the household she built over forty years. Its members disperse but continue calling themselves Seven Boats, redefining family as a promise rather than a structure.

## 35. Omi Shell-Chime — Fog Listener
**Personality:** Dreamy, shy, stubborn and acutely sensitive to sound.

- **Surface:** A blind harbour guide who locates boats through their shell chimes.
- **Private story:** Omi suspects the popular chime system disorients seals and has quietly altered several patterns.
- **Flood choice:** Dense ash turns day to night; everyone suddenly depends on the system she distrusts.
- **Arc:** She replaces constant chimes with call-and-response intervals, guiding humans while leaving silence for animals. Her method becomes Suun’s post-flood musical language.

## 36. Renn Drenn — Maru’s Daughter and Reed Diver
**Personality:** Daring, sarcastic, impatient and fiercely principled.

- **Surface:** A rescue diver who publicly condemns her father’s refugee policy.
- **Private story:** Renn enjoys moral opposition partly because it absolves her from acknowledging how his power benefits her.
- **Flood choice:** She must surrender her elite rescue place to a marsh pilot who knows drowned channels better.
- **Arc:** Renn moves from protest to relinquishment. She and Mele become rivals over how much compromised adults deserve a role in rebuilding.

## 37. Apo Kir — Clay-Disc Maker
**Personality:** Curious, absent-minded, conflict-avoidant and quietly jealous.

- **Surface:** An artisan firing magnetic navigation discs.
- **Private story:** Apo discovered the iron needles move unpredictably near certain northern stones but suppressed it because his deceased teacher’s reputation depends on the discs’ perfection.
- **Flood choice:** Revealing the flaw will invalidate half the armada’s instruments.
- **Arc:** He confesses and works with Seli to create redundant methods. He learns that honouring a teacher can mean correcting them.

## 38. Nalo Fen — Sturgeon Priest
**Personality:** Serene, theatrical, compassionate and politically cunning.

- **Surface:** Interpreter of the ancient sturgeons’ spring migration.
- **Private story:** Nalo has never believed the fish speak for gods; he uses ritual to enforce fishing limits.
- **Flood choice:** Sturgeon flee south at the wrong season. He can call it divine warning despite private disbelief.
- **Arc:** His “false” prophecy saves fishers. Nalo ends unsure whether usefulness makes a message sacred, while followers become more certain than ever.

## 39. Dessa Fen — Net-Mender
**Personality:** Practical, skeptical, affectionate and incapable of leaving a question alone.

- **Surface:** Nalo’s sister, forever mocking priestly performance.
- **Private story:** Dessa secretly restores discarded ritual nets because their patterns contain generations of mathematical knowledge.
- **Flood choice:** Fishing nets are needed as rescue ropes, destroying encoded patterns.
- **Arc:** She unravels them while teaching each knot aloud. Ritual survives as communal memory rather than object, bringing her closer to the brother she dismissed.

## 40. Venn-of-No-Father — Competitive Swimmer
**Personality:** Magnetic, vain, brave and unexpectedly gentle with the weak.

- **Surface:** Suun’s champion swimmer and favourite subject of harbour songs.
- **Private story:** Venn fabricated a prestigious father because fatherlessness carries no legal shame but does carry social mockery.
- **Flood choice:** He can win the ceremonial crossing that secures a rescue command or stop mid-race for a rival in distress.
- **Arc:** He stops. Losing status frees him from the invented identity. He coordinates swimmers who tether boats together during the breach and dies after saving thirty-two people.

## 41. Kiri Drenn — Maru’s Mother
**Personality:** Charming, forgetful, piercingly insightful and unpredictable.

- **Surface:** An elder whose failing memory is treated as pleasant confusion.
- **Private story:** Kiri remembers old songs about a previous lake breach but cannot retain the present long enough to explain them consistently.
- **Flood choice:** The family considers leaving her because evacuation requires speed.
- **Arc:** Maru takes her. Her fragmented song reveals a dry ridge under shallow water, saving their boat. Some call her prophet; the truth is that a discarded elderly memory mattered.

## 42. Hesh Blue-Oar — Boatwright
**Personality:** Pessimistic, meticulous, loyal and darkly hilarious.

- **Surface:** A builder who predicts every vessel will sink and is usually wrong.
- **Private story:** Hesh’s first boat did sink, killing his twin. Every later hull contains a hidden piece of the twin’s oar.
- **Flood choice:** Speed requires boats leave before his full inspection.
- **Arc:** He teaches passengers emergency repair rather than trying to guarantee perfection. His ugliest, most criticized cargo boat becomes the principal survivor vessel.

## 43. Saan Lio — Debt Collector
**Personality:** Polite, relentless, lonely and secretly sentimental.

- **Surface:** The person families hide from when his blue sail arrives.
- **Private story:** Saan keeps tokens from every debt he forgave, though public mercy would cost his office.
- **Flood choice:** Boat places are allocated according to debt status, and he controls the ledgers.
- **Arc:** He drops every debt tablet into the lake and claims the water destroyed them. Afterward, former debtors discover his forgiveness tokens and finally understand him—too late, because Saan dies in the first wave.

## 44. Luma Reed — Marsh Ambassador
**Personality:** Formal, proud, observant and slow to trust.

- **Surface:** Representative of communities the four powers call “temporary settlements.”
- **Private story:** Luma left her own child, Kes, with relatives while pursuing recognition and fears ambition made her an absent mother.
- **Flood choice:** The Accord offers marsh citizenship only if she remains in Tavra as negotiator while evacuation begins.
- **Arc:** She stays, winning legal recognition but missing the flood that separates her from Kes. They reunite in the final pages, neither able to resume the relationship they imagined. They begin again as strangers who choose kinship.

## 45. Piri Small-Reed — Apprentice Cook
**Personality:** Anxious, generous, inventive and always hungry.

- **Surface:** A galley child who burns every traditional dish.
- **Private story:** Piri eats compulsively because of early famine and hides food in boat walls, causing rot.
- **Flood choice:** He must reveal the dangerous caches before launch.
- **Arc:** After confessing, he invents thin smoked food tiles that store safely and feed hundreds. He keeps one private tile through the whole flood and gives it to Tiri on the first hungry morning after.

# KHELED — THE SUNWARD PLAIN

## 46. Aven Tal — Moon-Court Mediator · Principal POV
**Personality:** Eloquent, analytical, compassionate and afraid of decisive action.

- **Surface:** Kheled’s finest practitioner of arguing every side.
- **Private story:** Aven once delayed judgment in a water dispute until violence made the decision for her. She believes certainty is arrogance, but indecision has become its own arrogance.
- **Flood choice:** She holds the deciding voice on compulsory evacuation.
- **Arc:** She votes no, hoping for more evidence, and spends the rest of the story living inside that consequence. During the flood she stops mediating and commands the moon court opened as refuge. She survives without being granted moral redemption.

## 47. Tarek Os — Keeper of Deep Water · Principal POV
**Personality:** Methodical, humble, stubborn and uncomfortable with praise.

- **Surface:** A cistern engineer who thinks in gradients, pressure and stone.
- **Private story:** Tarek’s husband died by suicide after a public engineering failure; Tarek now hides every sign of his own exhaustion.
- **Flood choice:** He can strengthen Kheled’s cisterns or turn them into overflow chambers that will deliberately contaminate sacred drinking water but absorb the first surge.
- **Arc:** He chooses contamination, saving the lower city but creating a year-long water crisis. He learns to let others share responsibility and becomes one of rebuilding’s exhausted, living founders rather than another silent casualty.

## 48. Nema Vey — Astronomer of the Ninth Stair
**Personality:** Acerbic, disciplined, curious and contemptuous of popularity.

- **Surface:** The scholar proving that unusual tides are not caused by the moon.
- **Private story:** Nema manipulated observations early in her career to discredit a rival and has never confessed.
- **Flood choice:** Her old rival is now the only person able to verify the flood model; inviting scrutiny could expose both truth and fraud.
- **Arc:** She confesses before asking for help. Her reputation collapses, but the corrected joint model predicts the breach within one day. She becomes remembered incorrectly as a flawless prophet.

## 49. Ral Vey — Nema’s Rival
**Personality:** Amiable, patient, ambitious and capable of exquisite revenge.

- **Surface:** A provincial astronomer treated as Nema’s intellectual inferior.
- **Private story:** Ral discovered Nema’s fraud years ago and withheld proof, waiting for maximum advantage.
- **Flood choice:** Exposure during the crisis would give him the recognition he deserves but undermine the only credible warning.
- **Arc:** He validates the model first and reveals the fraud later. The choice saves thousands but leaves him furious that goodness feels so much like surrender.

## 50. Sava Ink-Palm — Archivist of the Open Court · Principal POV
**Personality:** Curious, possessive, compassionate and ruthlessly organized.

- **Surface:** Custodian of Kheled’s clay, textile and oral records.
- **Private story:** Sava has been quietly replacing damaged originals with perfect copies and failing to mark them, believing continuity matters more than authenticity.
- **Flood choice:** She receives sixty sled-loads for an archive that can carry only one. Laws? Medicine? Songs? Maps? Children’s games? Names of the dead?
- **Arc:** She first designs an elite canon, then burns the list and gives each departing household one thing to remember. The archive becomes distributed, altered and alive. Sava dies saving a box of blank tablets so survivors can record what happens next.

## 51. Daro In — Host of Three Nights
**Personality:** Gregarious, status-conscious, generous and quietly prejudiced.

- **Surface:** Keeper of Kheled’s largest guest house, famous for never refusing hospitality.
- **Private story:** Daro obeys the three-night law while ensuring poorer travellers feel unwelcome enough to leave early.
- **Flood choice:** Refugees arrive with nowhere to go after the legal third night.
- **Arc:** He initially expels them and loses his own house in a tremor hours later. Received by the people he rejected, Daro becomes a genuinely good host only after he owns no door.

## 52. Yara Os — Stone Hauler
**Personality:** Competitive, cheerful, physically fearless and emotionally evasive.

- **Surface:** Tarek’s younger sister and leader of the cistern crews.
- **Private story:** Yara was in love with Tarek’s husband and has blamed Tarek for not noticing his decline while refusing her own guilt.
- **Flood choice:** A collapse traps Tarek on one side and forty workers on the other.
- **Arc:** She saves the workers. Tarek survives by chance, denying her the punishment she expected. They finally speak honestly and rebuild as siblings without pretending grief is settled.

## 53. Halen Truth-Teller — Court Advocate
**Personality:** Principled, rigid, courageous and deficient in mercy.

- **Surface:** A lawyer famous for never lying, even ceremonially.
- **Private story:** Halen’s “honesty” often functions as permission to humiliate others.
- **Flood choice:** A frightened crowd asks whether the court is safe. The truthful answer is no, but panic would kill them immediately.
- **Arc:** He says, “It is safe for the next hundred breaths,” repeating the truth in survivable portions. Halen learns honesty is a duty to the listener, not an exhibition of purity.

## 54. Mina Dusk — Ochre Painter
**Personality:** Meditative, sensual, stubborn and unimpressed by authority.

- **Surface:** Painter of legal histories visible when dawn strikes court walls.
- **Private story:** Mina has inserted portraits of erased servants and labourers into official scenes for decades.
- **Flood choice:** She has one wall and one sunrise left before ash blocks the light.
- **Arc:** Mina paints ordinary people carrying one another, leaving rulers as tiny background marks. The wall survives underwater and becomes the future’s most misinterpreted image of “the gods escaping the deluge.”

## 55. Kesh Ar — Rain Caller
**Personality:** Sincere, anxious, humble and prone to mystical certainty under stress.

- **Surface:** A minor ritual specialist whose annual rain ceremony fails during drought.
- **Private story:** Kesh knows weather does not answer him but believes public ritual gives people a way to endure uncertainty.
- **Flood choice:** Desperate citizens ask him to call rain while the actual threat is water from the north.
- **Arc:** He transforms the ceremony into a departure blessing, openly saying he cannot control the sky. This honesty gives the evacuation emotional permission to begin.

## 56. Orra Tal — Aven’s Mother
**Personality:** Elegant, cutting, politically conservative and fiercely loving.

- **Surface:** Retired mediator who considers Aven too hesitant.
- **Private story:** Orra secretly arranged the exile of Aven’s violent father, then allowed Aven to believe he abandoned them.
- **Flood choice:** The exiled father arrives among refugees and threatens to expose her.
- **Arc:** Orra tells Aven first, destroying the idealized family story. She later chooses to rescue the man she exiled, not from forgiveness but because law must survive personal hatred.

## 57. Bel Aru — Salt-Pan Shepherd
**Personality:** Solitary, patient, literal and deeply amused by city customs.

- **Surface:** A rural herder dismissed as unsophisticated.
- **Private story:** Bel has tracked the movement of ground hornbills for thirty years and knows they nest only above ancient water marks.
- **Flood choice:** Officials seize his high pasture for nobles; he can conceal the safest ground out of spite.
- **Arc:** He marks the true line with white salt despite knowing elites will occupy it. Then he leads common families higher still, following the birds rather than the law. He becomes reluctant founder of a hill settlement.

## 58. Toma Reed-Ash — Funeral Cook
**Personality:** Tender, practical, irreverent and unable to refuse a request.

- **Surface:** Preparer of meals eaten after cremations.
- **Private story:** Toma keeps a spoonful from every funeral meal in sealed jars, terrified that forgetting a flavour means losing the person.
- **Flood choice:** The jars are too heavy for evacuation.
- **Arc:** She cooks all the saved portions into one impossible stew and feeds the departing city. Memory becomes something consumed and carried in bodies rather than stored on a shelf.

## 59. Kes Luma — Child of the Reed Margin · Principal POV
**Personality:** Curious, angry, inventive and allergic to pity.

- **Surface:** An eleven-year-old refugee who draws maps of places adults say never officially existed.
- **Private story:** Kes’s mother Luma left to advocate for the marsh people; he believes political importance mattered to her more than he did.
- **Flood choice:** He can board Pava’s privileged caravan alone or remain with the unregistered children he has gathered.
- **Arc:** He refuses individual rescue and guides the children using intimate marsh knowledge. His maps later become the foundation of the flood chronicle. Reunion with Luma is difficult, restrained and more honest than a sentimental embrace.

## 60. Oru the Roadless — Mammoth-Steppe Herdsman · Principal POV
**Personality:** Patient, humorous, self-sufficient and stubbornly nonpolitical.

- **Surface:** A wandering herder who refuses citizenship in any of the four powers.
- **Private story:** Oru’s people once guided Orun through a famine and were later denied winter pasture. He has taught his children that cities consume help and forget helpers.
- **Flood choice:** Mammoth matriarchs turn toward an unknown eastern pass. Oru can follow them with his family or warn the cities that despise him.
- **Arc:** He sends his family east and rides west alone with the warning. No council fully believes him, but individuals do. He survives by joining the final caravan, where he must accept that interdependence is not the same as submission.

## 61. Mara-of-No-Hearth — Itinerant Singer · Principal POV
**Personality:** Magnetic, evasive, compassionate, unreliable and privately desperate to belong.

- **Surface:** A road singer welcomed everywhere because she knows every culture's epics, scandal songs and ancestral genealogies.
- **Private story:** Mara claims she abandoned home for freedom. In truth, her Kheledi household expelled her after she altered a legal song to save her guilty younger sister. Since then, she has made contradiction into a philosophy so no one can accuse her of lying again.
- **Flood choice:** Leaders ask Mara to spread a simple, unified warning. The clean version will move people faster but erase uncertainty, local knowledge and the marsh communities already lost.
- **Arc:** She first tells each audience whatever will persuade them, creating mutually hostile explanations of the Flood. In the aftermath she refuses the rulers' official account and records incompatible testimonies side by side. She never finds one hearth; instead, she becomes the thread by which scattered hearths remember one another.

# 4. The connective cast: how sixty-one stories become one story

A large ensemble succeeds only when characters collide rather than waiting in separate biographies. These are the main connective chains.

## The Warning Chain
Korun notices cave lions leaving → Oru sees mammoths change route → Eira hears the basal fracture → Nema disproves lunar tides → Seli maps abnormal currents → Nara tastes salt in roots → Kes arrives with a map of the first drowned marsh.

No single clue proves the Flood. Together they do. The political tragedy is that no institution can assemble knowledge from a hunter, herder, scientist, navigator, gardener and child without ranking some voices beneath others.

## The Broken-Memory Chain
Kiri’s song describes an earlier lake breach → Dessa recognizes its rhythm in a ritual net → Sava finds a matching court fragment → Rovan’s clay maps identify the old channel → Tarek calculates where it will reach Kheled.

Each person possesses an incomplete piece. The solution requires oral, textile, ceramic, written and engineering knowledge to be treated as equal forms of record.

## The Refugee Chain
Kes loses the marsh → Maru denies entry → Pava denies kinship → Luma bargains for recognition → Daro enforces three-night hospitality → Olan invents the Fifth Hearth → Ama opens sacred tunnels.

The audience watches the word **refugee** move from abstraction to identity until almost everyone wears it.

## The Parent-and-Child Chain
Eira and Pell cannot share guilt; Seli and Tem fight over stolen recognition; Maru and Renn confuse opposition with independence; Luma and Kes confuse public duty with abandonment; Ama and Letha use tradition to avoid mourning.

The Flood cannot magically repair these families. It removes the luxury of postponing their truth.

## The Things-We-Carry Chain
Sava chooses memories; Nara chooses plants; Iso chooses a wick; Rovan breaks map-pots; Toma cooks remembrance; Vessa shares grain; Piri preserves food; Nemi invents transport.

Together they answer the story’s central question: civilization is neither treasure nor bloodline. It is a set of practised relationships.

# 5. Chapter plan

## PART ONE — HAIRLINE CRACKS

### 1. The Wrong Note
Eira hears a deep pulse arrive seven breaths earlier than expected. Pell’s concert above masks the second pulse. Eira says nothing until she can repeat the measurement.

### 2. A Shore That Moved
Seli finds a navigation marker inland from the waterline and assumes theft. Kiri sings a verse about “the lake that steps backward before running.”

### 3. Salt in the Orange Root
Nara tastes a dying root and detects salt. Ossa orders the tree burned before other gardeners see it.

### 4. The Animals Face East
Oru’s mammoths abandon spring grass. Korun finds lion tracks moving in the same impossible direction.

### 5. A Country Without a Name
Kes’s marsh floods under a clear sky. His grandmother dies securing children in reed rafts. Official Suun reports call the place “unoccupied seasonal wetland.”

### 6. The First Table
Representatives gather in Tavra. Ilyon makes everyone feel heard while committing to nothing. Aven is appointed neutral examiner. Simo leaks an exaggerated version of Eira’s warning.

### 7. The Demonstration
Eira attempts to reproduce the sound before leaders. Varo’s followers sing outside; a damaged instrument produces a false note. Sura exposes the equipment error. Eira is publicly discredited.

### 8. Two Coats
Tiri steals Aven’s outer coat and discovers evacuation drafts in its lining. She thinks they are maps to treasure and runs.

### 9. The Station Falls
A remote Oruni listening house vanishes. Tovan mounts an unauthorized rescue and finds the building intact fifty metres below the ice, with lamps still burning.

## PART TWO — THE ARGUMENT OF CITIES

### 10. Three Nights
First marsh refugees reach Kheled. Daro hosts them exactly three nights and then closes the doors. Kes begins drawing every door that refuses him.

### 11. The Price of Panic
Ilyon shows Nara casualty records from his childhood evacuation. She understands his fear but tells him fear cannot be public policy.

### 12. The Fish Turn South
Aruun sturgeon migrate out of season. Nalo declares a warning he does not believe. Dessa discovers the old net pattern.

### 13. Mother’s Bones
Ama refuses to open name-caves for geological inspection. Letha arrives from Tavra and turns the dispute into eighteen years of family grief before the entire council.

### 14. The Fifth Hearth
Olan fabricates an Oruni lineage for steppe migrants. Var discovers the fraud and blackmails him for access to restricted maps.

### 15. The Honest Fraud
Nema confesses to Ral and asks him to verify her tide model. He agrees, planning to expose her after the vote.

### 16. A Vessel Is a Promise
Maru chains off Suun’s eastern harbour. Renn cuts one chain. Father and daughter turn refugee policy into public family warfare.

### 17. The Moon Court
Every principal viewpoint converges in Kheled. Evidence remains incomplete. Aven votes against compulsory evacuation by one voice. Kes shouts the names of drowned marsh villages until guards remove him.

### 18. The Failed Channel
Tarek and Letha attempt to relieve Lake Aruun through an ancient spillway. Sabotage is suspected, but the failure is geological: stone thought solid is fractured. The intervention speeds erosion toward the final breach.

## PART THREE — FORTY DAYS OF DUST

### 19. Ash Bloom
Tavra’s volcano produces a low eruption. Ash closes the sky and mirror gardens begin dying. Cala’s animals panic before each tremor.

### 20. All Debts Remembered
Saan destroys debt records. Simo exposes the elite cavern list before alternatives are ready. Riots break Vessa’s hidden grain store open.

### 21. The City Becomes a Bell
Pell retunes Orun’s halls. When the glacier next moves, every wall hums. Denial becomes physically impossible.

### 22. One Thousand Objects
Sava stages a public argument over what enters the archive. A child asks why there are no games on the list. She begins to understand the list is wrong.

### 23. The Seven Boats
Iri cuts her flotilla apart. Hesh launches uninspected hulls. Omi redesigns the fog-call. Suun turns its whole society into a moving fleet.

### 24. The Last Orchard
Nara takes common cuttings. Ossa’s diverted channels collapse, burying workers. Mele guides the rescue through irrigation theft-routes.

### 25. The White Line
Bel marks the ancient high-water boundary. Nobles occupy it. Ground hornbills nest higher.

### 26. The Confession Road
Var tells Eira the truth about their mother. She does not forgive him. They must continue walking together anyway.

### 27. The Feast of Every Funeral
Toma cooks her memory jars. Jori drums. Kesh changes the rain ritual into farewell. Kheled eats its dead and leaves home.

### 28. The Lake Steps Back
Lake Aruun drops suddenly, boats settling in mud. Seli orders everyone away from the exposed shore. Thousands walk onto it to gather stranded fish.

## PART FOUR — WHEN THE HORIZON ROSE

### 29. Day Zero: The Blue Shelf Breaks
The glacial lake escapes beneath Orun. Eira loses her hearing. Tovan chooses the rope crossing over the noble rescue. Varo reverses his pilgrims.

### 30. Day One: The River Without Banks
Water tears through steppe valleys. Oru guides people by following mammoths. Olan’s invented Fifth Hearth becomes real through shared labour.

### 31. Day Two: The Inland Sea Empties
Aruun breaches south. Tem rejects Seli’s route and is lost after saving his passengers. Venn swims the tether line. Saan’s blue sail disappears.

### 32. Day Three: The Wall Opens
The first western tsunami reaches Tavra. Dar opens the sea gates. Tal sends the final mirror signal. Beren keeps ovens burning until water extinguishes them.

### 33. Day Four: The Moon Court Fills
Tarek floods sacred cisterns deliberately. Yara chooses forty workers over her brother. Halen gives truth one hundred breaths at a time.

### 34. Day Five: No Highest Place
The marked high-water line fails. Bel follows hornbills. Refugees must abandon carts, archives and ancestral remains. Sava distributes memory objects.

### 35. Day Six: The Covered Water
Nemi’s broad sled-boats cross a flooded valley. Tiri gives away her second coat. Piri gives away his hidden food. Small relinquishments become the moral centre of the epic.

### 36. Day Seven: The Uncounted
Kes refuses solitary rescue. Maru dismantles his harbour to reach the children. Pava acknowledges Kes publicly as kin, but he boards only after every child has a place.

### 37. Day Eight: Silence After Water
The wave sequence ends. Ash still falls. Eira cannot hear the survivors calling. She begins drawing their mouths and hands.

### 38. The First Morning
The refugees expect water to recede. It does not. Nara plants cuttings in foreign soil. Iso lights no sacred flame; everyone warms the wick between their hands.

### 39. The Trial of the Living
Survivors demand blame. Rusk confesses suppression, Nema confesses fraud, Aven confesses her vote and Ilyon confesses delay. No single villain satisfies the crowd because responsibility is distributed.

### 40. Many Floods
Mara-of-No-Hearth asks each camp what happened. One says divine punishment, one says an ice beast broke its chain, one says animals warned the innocent, one says engineers angered the lake, one says no one listened to a child.

### 41. The Blank Tablets
Sava dies protecting unwritten clay. Kes and Tiri press the names of lost places into it—not in official script, but with marsh-map marks and glove patterns.

### 42. One Year Later
Four successor communities have already changed the story. Ama finally sings every name regardless of season. Luma finds Kes; he offers her a blank tablet instead of an embrace.

### 43. Epilogue: Twenty Thousand Years
A fragment of Rovan’s map-pot lies on a modern seabed beside Nemi’s sled runner and Mina’s painted wall. Its meaning is unknowable. Over darkness, contradictory survivor voices tell the beginning of the Flood. None is complete. None is entirely false.

# 6. End-state ledger

The story should not imply that moral worth determines survival.

## Characters who die during or soon after the Flood
- **Tovan** — keeps the rope crossing open.
- **Ossa** — delayed death from ash-lung after revealing the water maps.
- **Dar** — opens Tavra’s sea gates.
- **Tem** — saves passengers after rejecting Seli’s route.
- **Venn** — completes the armada tether.
- **Saan** — disappears after erasing debts.
- **Sava** — protects blank tablets, not old treasures.

## Characters whose fate remains disputed in later legend
- **Varo** — followers claim he ascended into ice; Eira saw him swept away.
- **Mina** — her wall survives, but witnesses disagree whether she escaped.
- **Jori** — a final drum continues after his tunnel floods; it may be echo, not life.

## Characters who survive but lose status
- Rusk, Ilyon, Nema, Maru, Hadran, Aven and Pava.

## Characters who become founders despite lacking former authority
- Nara, Nemi, Deya, Cala, Seli, Omi, Dessa, Tarek, Bel, Mele, Kes and Tiri.

## Characters who survive without neat reconciliation
- Eira and Var.
- Letha and Ama.
- Maru and Renn.
- Luma and Kes.
- Aven and Orra.
- Ilyon and Beren.

This matters: catastrophe creates urgency, not emotional magic.

# 7. Personality and voice guide

To keep sixty-one people distinct, assign each a verbal rhythm and stress behaviour.

- **Eira** speaks in corrections; under stress she becomes poetic against her will.
- **Tovan** turns commands into jokes; near death he becomes plain.
- **Nara** uses plant metaphors only when she trusts someone.
- **Ilyon** remembers everyone’s name but avoids the word “I.”
- **Seli** describes currents as shapes and interrupts bad questions.
- **Maru** uses numbers to make moral choices sound inevitable.
- **Aven** says “on the other hand” until the flood removes the other hand.
- **Tarek** explains structures through touch, never abstraction.
- **Oru** answers questions with observations about animals.
- **Kes** asks adults to name places they call empty.
- **Sava** categorizes even arguments; late in the story she embraces fragments.
- **Mara** never tells the same story in the same way twice.

Humour should remain present. People approaching catastrophe still flirt, complain about food, misunderstand one another, celebrate birthdays, resent siblings and make terrible jokes. Constant solemnity would make the world feel less real.

---

# 8. Rules for writing every person as a person

1. **No one exists only to die for another character’s development.** Every death resolves the dying person’s own choice.
2. **No villain knows they are the villain.** Rusk prevents panic; Maru protects capacity; Ilyon protects civic trust; Ossa protects food confidence.
3. **Correct people may behave badly.** Eira is right but poor at persuasion; Nema is right after committing fraud; Simo exposes genuine injustice recklessly.
4. **Wrong people may be admirable.** Aven’s caution comes from respect; Dar’s faith in the wall comes from duty; Tem saves lives after making a fatal error.
5. **Children alter events rather than symbolize innocence.** Kes maps; Tiri carries information; Tal signals; Piri solves food storage.
6. **Elders are neither obstacles nor automatic sages.** Kiri’s memory matters, Hadran resists change, Ama changes law, Ossa conceals failure.
7. **Disability is not prophecy.** Omi, Cala, Jori and Eira have specific knowledge shaped by their bodies, not magical compensation.
8. **Love does not erase harm.** Reconciliation may begin, fail, or remain unfinished.
9. **Every culture contains disagreement.** No region has one worldview, class interest or moral character.
10. **Survival is collective and unfair.** Preparation matters, chance matters, geography matters and privilege matters.

# 9. The final emotional image

On the first anniversary, survivors gather above a coastline that no longer resembles any map. Each person carries a small cup of water from where they now live. They pour the cups into a shared stone basin.

Ama begins the old Oruni song of names. Nalo adds a Suun harbour rhythm. Jori’s students answer by striking the earth. Kesh speaks no prayer, only the weather. Nara plants a bitter-orange cutting beside the basin. Kes interrupts the ceremony to correct the name of his drowned village.

No one silences him.

Eira, now deaf, watches sixty-one mouths shape sixty-one versions of the old world. She understands that memory will alter, simplify and contradict itself. She opens Sava’s last blank tablet and draws the horizon—not where it is, but where every survivor remembers it.

The book ends with the sentence:

> **Before it became a legend, the end of the world happened to everyone differently.**
